Bailu Hub: your questions answered

Bailu Hub is an app for learning and practising Chinese internal martial arts, published by Shanghai Bailuxing Data Technology Co., Ltd. Read lesson explanations, study 3D demonstrations, practise as directed, record reflections and ask concrete questions with AI assistance. The initial core experience is planned as a free release on November 13, 2026, subject to review and regional availability.

Published and maintained by Shanghai Bailuxing Data Technology Co., Ltd. · Updated：2026-11-01

Are Bailu Hub and 白研社 the same product?

Yes. Bailu Hub is the current English product name, and 白研社 is its Chinese name. The full Chinese website name is 白鹿行研习社. The international website is www.bailuhub.com; the mainland China website is www.bailuhub.cn.

What is the first lesson?

The first lesson is Feel Your Dantian — The Bellows Practice (丹田初动·拉风箱). Adapted by Bailu Hub from the Xin Yi Quan Squatting Monkey practice, it introduces dantian opening and closing and vertical body connections. Study the explanation and demonstration before practising as directed.

What do the 3D demonstrations help with?

The app lets you study movements and structure from different angles alongside written explanations. Website animations and screenshots are previews. They do not mean that the app automatically tracks, scores or certifies your movement as correct.

How do I begin my first session?

Choose the first lesson, read its explanation, prepare your space and device, study the demonstration and follow that session’s guidance, then record what you noticed. Use End Training when you need to finish. Do not add training load simply to improve a count.

What are practice notes for?

Record specific sensations, changes and questions so you can review your own progress and know what to observe next time. A short, honest note is enough; there is no need to exaggerate results or reach a conclusion after every session.

Can AI explanations replace an in-person teacher?

AI helps explain lesson material, movement principles and concrete questions. It does not replace in-person observation or provide medical diagnosis. An AI explanation is not proof that a movement is correct or that you should increase training load.
